# Percussion notation: the dependency licence check (RFC #10)

RFC #10 proposes percussion in three layers — live hits, a 16-step grid, and last
a Strudel-style text notation — and flags one decision before the notation is
built:

> **Dependency vs in-house.** Strudel/TidalCycles are a real, permissively
> licensed ecosystem, and a subset is small. But Etherphone carries Gesture
> Synth's non-commercial licence, so a new dependency's terms need a deliberate
> look before it is adopted. […] A small in-house subset — steps, rests (`~`),
> maybe `[x y]` groups and `*n` repeats — may fit better than a full pattern
> language.

and then, under open questions: _"If notation is adopted, is it a dependency or
a subset written here? (Licence check required either way.)"_

This is that check. **Conclusion: take neither dependency. Write the subset.**

## What the licences actually are

Read from the licence files themselves, not from summaries:

| Project     | Licence      | Source read                                                                            |
| ----------- | ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Strudel     | **AGPL-3.0** | its `LICENSE`; the project has moved upstream to Codeberg (`codeberg.org/uzu/strudel`) |
| TidalCycles | **GPL-3.0**  | `tidalcycles/Tidal` → `LICENSE`                                                        |

The RFC's own wording — "permissively licensed ecosystem" — is the part that does
not hold up. Neither is permissive. Both are strong copyleft.

## Why neither can be taken

Three clauses matter, and two of them are the same objection from different
angles.

**1. AGPL-3.0 §5(c) — the whole work becomes AGPL.** A work based on the Program
must be licensed _entirely_ under AGPL to anyone who receives a copy. Etherphone
is a single-page application; the notation parser would be linked into the same
bundle that plays the chords. There is no separable-bundle argument to make.

**2. AGPL-3.0 §13 — the network clause, and this app is exactly its target.**
Running a modified version so users interact with it remotely obliges you to
offer those users the Corresponding Source. A web instrument _is_ remote network
interaction. This is compatible with being open source — this repository already
is — but not with the next point.

**3. Both licences forbid the restriction this project already carries.**
AGPL-3.0 §10 and §7 forbid imposing further restrictions on the rights granted.
Gesture Synth's licence, which this project is bound by as a derivative, is
**non-commercial**: selling the app, a paid service, or a paid product requires
Eric Wei's permission. That requirement is a further restriction. You cannot
satisfy both, and §12 says what happens then: you may not convey the work at all.

That is the whole answer. It is not that AGPL is inconvenient here — it is that
the two sets of terms **contradict**, and the resolution is not to combine them.

GPL-3.0 (Tidal) fails on 1 and 3 as well; it lacks §13, but that does not help.

## What this does not forbid

- **The notation's syntax is not the obstacle.** Mini-notation is a text format —
  `bd ~ sd ~`, `[x y]`, `*4` — and writing an independent parser for a small
  subset of a text syntax is not a copying of the Tidal/Strudel implementation.
  What must not be copied is their _source_.
- **Studying them is fine.** Reading Tidal's documentation to understand the
  semantics is exactly how a compatible subset gets designed.
- **Reference in prose is fine.** "In the spirit of Tidal's mini-notation" is
  nominative, and both projects are widely referenced that way.

## Consequences for #10

1. **Build the in-house subset**, scoped as the RFC proposes: steps, rests (`~`),
   groups (`[x y]`), and repeats (`*n`). That is a parser over a hair under two
   hundred lines and needs no dependency at all — the same shape as
   `src/audio/wavetable.ts`, and Node-testable.
2. **Say so in the credits.** The percussion section should credit TidalCycles
   and Strudel nominatively for the idiom, the way the README already credits
   Gesture Synth and MiMu. Being unaffiliated and unlicensed-by-them is worth
   stating, not hiding.
3. **Keep the kit ours.** RFC #10 already requires the drum samples to be
   generated by `scripts/generate-samples.mjs` rather than sourced. That is the
   same defence as the notation one, and it also sidesteps the sample-bank
   licensing that Strudel's default banks carry separately.
4. **Add a licence note to the credits if anything changes.** If a future
   dependency is taken, §5(c) and §10 mean it must be permissive — MIT, BSD,
   Apache-2.0 — and must not forbid commercial use.
